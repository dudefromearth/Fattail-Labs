## The four asks

### 1. Closed + last print: respectful, not alarming?

**Yes.**

Echo, closed row:

> Badge / plane: **Off market**  
> Package / curve: *The numeric **last print**, labeled held. Curve is the last known print. Member reads: last known print — the market is closed.*  
> Must not say: *Live; Pre/post; unavailable; OPF unavailable; error; offline; broken; “no data”; “market down”; a blank cell*

Echo, HIG:

> **No pastel panic.** Closed, held, expired, and no-print are expected options states — not alerts, not red banners, not “connection lost.”  
> **Last print is not an outage.** Off market + a number is truth. Do not flash **OPF unavailable**.

Tango: this is the sentence a bleeding member needed. “Off market” is calm. It does not shout CLOSED, DOWN, or ERROR. Pairing a **number** with “last known print — the market is closed” is respectful: the product still has something true to show, and it does not pretend the feed died. The must-not-say list is the right panic dictionary.

**Tango opinion (not a return):** in the same row Echo writes “labeled held.” Echo’s own two-axis rule says the chip quality word is **last print**, and that we must not say “Closed” on the plane and “Held” on the chip. A Saturday 45-DTE last print is not the Law C **Held residual** window. If W3-2 prints the word **held** under an Off-market number, a tired member can fuse “market closed” with “this option settled.” Keep the chip qualifier **last print**. Leave **held** / **HELD RESIDUAL** on the between-clocks row. Echo already named last print as “the package-quality phrase under a number when OPF has a held print (`extended` or `closed`)” — this note only stops the closed chip from borrowing the residual token.

**Tango opinion:** “Off market” can, in civilian English, sound like “this thing is off.” The member-reads line (*the market is closed*) is what prevents that. W3-2 must not ship the badge without that quality phrase nearby. Do not “fix” it to **Closed** or **Unavailable** — those are more alarming, and **Off market** is Coach’s six-word list.

### 2. Held/residual: cannot be mistaken for live?

**Yes — if W3-2 ships the pair Echo wrote (named state + number), not the number alone.**

Echo, held / residual row:

> Badge / plane: **Held residual**  
> Package / curve: *Frozen last print / residual **plus** the named state. Numeric is allowed (Coach 2026-08-16). Curve is residual, never live. Member reads: settled; still on the card until midnight ET; not live.*  
> Package-cell token: **HELD RESIDUAL**  
> Must not say: *Live; open; “still trading”; “through the close”; “expires at the bell”; “expires at 4”; last print presented as current tape; a blank cell*

Echo, HIG:

> **Held residual is never Live.** After τ and before midnight ET the plane may still show a number; that number is residual.

Tango: the must-not-say list is the live-leak fence. “Still trading,” “through the close,” and “expires at 4” are exactly the stories a bleeding 0-DTE trader tells themselves after the bell. Echo forbids them. The badge **Held residual** plus ALL-CAPS **HELD RESIDUAL** is a different dialect from **Live**. A glance at a naked number would still read as live — Echo does not allow the number without the named state. That pairing is what Tango is approving.

**Tango opinion:** *settled* is true for the τ clock and dangerous in the member’s ear. A bleeding trader hears “settled” as “it’s over — this number is what you got.” Held residual is **not** the EXPIRED ghost and **not** a result. Prefer the rest of Echo’s own sentence: *still on the card until midnight ET; not live.* Keep **Eastern Time** (never “midnight,” never the member’s kitchen clock). That is Echo’s Law C, restated for dignity.

**Tango opinion:** *Frozen last print* is clear to us and can sound like the UI froze (B1: the app is broken). Implementer note is fine; the member-facing words should stay **Held residual** + **last print** / residual, not “frozen.”

Coach’s permission to show a number here is intact. Tango is not asking to blank it.

### 3. EXPIRED + defined debit: honest, not a sales number?

**Yes — the number is the structure’s defined debit, labeled, on a ghost. It is not a result.**

Echo, EXPIRED row:

> Badge / plane: **Expired**  
> Package / curve: *Named state **EXPIRED** plus the **defined debit** on the viewport ghost. Never a blank price. Member reads: this expiration day has ended (after midnight Eastern Time). The ghost is the defined debit, not a live mark.*  
> Must not say: *Live; last print as a current mark; a blank cell; **$0** as “worthless”; “you made / you lost”; profit theater*

Echo, HIG:

> **No profit theater.** No P&L celebration, no “you’re winning,” no lift-now urgency on a last print or a ghost.  
> **EXPIRED is never a blank price.** Ghost + defined debit, or the named state **EXPIRED** — never an empty cell that looks like a bug.

Tango: this is honest process language. **Defined debit** is what the member pointed at, not what the tape paid them. Forbidding “you made / you lost,” forbidding **$0** as “worthless,” and forbidding a blank cell are the three ways this row could have become either a sales number or a panic blank. Echo closed all three.

**Tango opinion:** a naked dollar amount on an expired card *will* be read as P&L or as a live mark. W3-2 may not put the number on the ghost without the words **defined debit** (and the **EXPIRED** name). No green/red celebration, no plus-sign theater, no “max loss realized.” Defined debit is a definition, not a score.

**Tango opinion:** “worthless” is correctly banned even when the residual is zero. A bleeding member does not need the product to pronounce their trade dead. **EXPIRED** + defined debit is enough.
