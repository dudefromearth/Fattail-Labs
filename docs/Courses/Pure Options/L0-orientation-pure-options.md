# Pure Options

*Orientation — The Pure Look*

Design your thesis. Design your risk. Design your payoff.

That's the course. Three decisions, all of them yours, all of them made before you commit a
dollar. Everything in the modules ahead is learning to make them well.

### What this course is

An option is the only instrument in the market where the shape of the outcome is yours to
build.

Buy a stock and you accept the payoff the stock offers — a straight line. Up in proportion,
down in proportion. You choose whether to be in it and how much to hold. You don't choose
what happens next.

An option is different in kind. You decide, before you enter, the most you can lose. You
decide where the position starts paying and where it stops. You decide whether the outcome is
a line, a step, or a shape that pays most in a region you nominate. All of it is set at
construction, by choosing which contracts to hold and which to sell against them.

That's what options are for. Not a cheaper way to buy stock. Not rent collected on shares. A
set of components from which you build the payoff you actually want.

Most options education never gets there. It teaches the components and then spends the rest
of its time using them to end up owning stock — covered calls, cash-secured puts, the wheel,
assignment. That approach is real and people run it seriously. It's built for a different
clock than yours.

**This course is for traders who work in days, not months.**

### What's assumed

This isn't an introduction. You should already know what a call and a put are, what a strike
is, and how an option is quoted. No lesson here defines those.

You're arriving by one of two roads.

**You recently finished a beginner options course.** You have the vocabulary, and you were
pointed toward ownership. That orientation answers a question you're not asking.

**You've been trading options for a while.** Nothing you learned was wrong exactly, but the
strategies never quite fit, and you've been adjusting them ever since. Widening strikes,
rolling out, shifting the timing — small corrections to something that almost worked.

Either road arrives at the same place, and the course is built to be read from both.

### What's out of scope

Four things, set down deliberately and not picked up again:

- Share ownership
- Assignment
- Covered-call income
- The wheel

Not because they're illegitimate. Because every one of them is a long-horizon posture, and
you trade a short-horizon clock.

There's a benefit to setting them down now rather than later. Each of those strategies trains
you to think about options in terms of what stock you'll end up holding, and that habit
quietly shapes how you read every position afterward. It's difficult to unlearn once formed.
Don't build it, and you never have to dismantle it.

### How the course is built

Three modules, ten lessons.

**See It.** An option doesn't live on a line — it lives in a room with three walls: price,
time and volatility. You'll learn to split an option's price into its two parts, read a risk
graph as something that changes shape as the clock runs, and see what happens to all three
dimensions when the term gets short.

**Build It.** Time decay is a bill to one side of a trade and a collection to the other.
You'll learn why the obvious way to collect it is the wrong kind of risk, how one structure
collects it with the danger capped, and how to screen a live chain to find one.

**Run It.** What actually happens when you deploy this — including how often it resolves as
the loss you defined. That's the design working, not failing, and it's why the unit of
judgment is a campaign rather than a trade.

Each lesson has a page, a short video, and a drill you do on a live option chain. **Do the
drills.** They're observational — nothing in this course asks you to place a trade — but they
are where the reading turns into seeing.

### How to work through it

The order is load-bearing. Each lesson is built on the one before it, and two in particular
will be tempting to skip.

The risk graph lesson comes after the two-values lesson, not before, because you can't read a
picture until you know what it's drawing. And the structure doesn't appear until Module 2,
because a structure you can't read is just a shape someone told you to trust.

If you find yourself wanting to jump ahead to the butterfly, that impulse is the thing this
course is designed to correct. The structure is easy to copy and worthless to copy.

### What this course won't give you

No signals. No trade alerts. No setups to copy.

If you want someone to tell you what to trade, this is the wrong room, and no amount of
working through it will turn it into the right one.

What you'll have at the end is the ability to look at a chain yourself, form a view, build a
position shaped like that view, know your worst case before you enter, and decide whether
what's on offer is worth taking. Including — often — deciding that it isn't.

**More control. Not more risk.**

### Where this sits

Pure Options is the first rung of three. It teaches the instrument and the discipline to
construct with it.

Running a live session under pressure is **0DTE Foundations**. The complete framework is

### Start here

Open Module 1 and read the first lesson. Then open your broker and do the drill.

That second part isn't optional. You can read this whole course in an afternoon and retain
almost none of it. The drills are where it lands.
