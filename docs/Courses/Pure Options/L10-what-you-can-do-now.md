# Lesson 10 — What you can do now, and what you cannot yet

*Pure Options · Module 3: Run It*

Most courses end by telling you what you've achieved. This one is going to tell you both halves,
because the second half is more useful and almost nobody writes it down.

Start with what's real.

### What you can do now

**You can read an option for what it is.** Its price splits into two parts, and you know which
forces move each one. You know that intrinsic answers to price alone, and that time and
volatility both live inside the extrinsic part. An option is no longer a directional bet with
extra vocabulary attached.

**You can read a risk graph properly.** Two lines, not one. You know the gap between them is
the extrinsic value, that it closes as the term runs out, and that the closing is where most of
what matters happens. You can look at a position and see what it's worth now, separately from
what it would be worth at expiration.

**You know what a short term does.** Time accelerates, price sharpens, and volatility's
exposure shrinks while its consequence grows. You know that third one is the counterintuitive
one, and you'd catch someone stating it wrongly.

**You can choose your side of the clock deliberately.** Decay is a flow with two ends, and you
know the shape of both positions — the buyer's cost of being early, the seller's collection and
its unbounded obligation. You wouldn't take the second one unprotected.

**You can build a structure rather than pick one.** Two spreads sharing a body, sold middle
harvesting decay, bought wings capping the damage, nothing lost but the debit. You know why
it's four contracts and not two, what the control surface gives you, and why the broken wing —
which improves nearly every number — gets declined anyway.

**You can screen.** Width from where you are on the volatility spectrum. Placement around the
expected move, where the crowd thins. Price last, five to ten percent of width or no trade.
And you know why that order can't be reversed.

**You can say no.** Which is most of it. You can look at a full chain and conclude that nothing
on it qualifies today, and know that's the screen working.

**You know what to expect.** Half the time you lose the debit, by design. The gain comes from
the transit rather than the destination. A single loss carries no information, and the unit of
judgment is a campaign inside a bounded allocation, never a trade.

That's not a small list. Read it again and notice how much of it is *seeing* rather than doing
— because that's what this course was for.

### What you cannot do yet

**You have not run a live session under pressure.** Everything here was observational. Knowing
the procedure and executing it while price moves, spreads widen and the clock runs is a
different skill, and the gap between them is larger than it feels right now. That's what
**0DTE Foundations** teaches, and it isn't a formality.

**You have not managed a position through a violent transit.** Lesson 8 said management is the
trade. It didn't teach you to manage — it taught you *that* management is where the outcome
lives. Reading a trail, deciding when to take what's on offer, acting when conditions are worst:
none of that is in this course.

**You do not have a method.** You have a structure and a screen. A method also specifies width
selection within a regime, directional bias, debit discipline as an ongoing practice, and the
profit-management trail. That's **The Convexity Edge**, and it's a body of work rather than a
missing chapter.

**You have not run a campaign to completion.** You wrote a plan for one you weren't going to
run. Running it — through a quiet stretch, through a run of designed losses, without sizing up
— is the thing itself, and it takes as long as it takes.

**You have not traded any of this.** Deliberately. Nothing in this course asked you to place a
trade, and if you've been doing so alongside it, that was your decision and not this course's
instruction.

### Why the second list is the more valuable one

A course that only tells you what you've gained produces a specific failure: someone who feels
equipped, deploys, meets the gap between reading and doing at the worst possible moment, and
concludes the method doesn't work.

The method works. It just has prerequisites this course doesn't supply, and knowing precisely
where its edge is means you can decide what to do next rather than discovering it with money on
the table.

So the second list isn't a sales pitch for the next rung. It's the same honesty the rest of the
course ran on. **If the only thing you take from Pure Options is that you can now tell the
difference between what you understand and what you don't, that's worth the time.** A trader who
knows the boundary of their competence is already ahead of most.

### What to do with this

Three things, in order.

**Keep doing the drills.** Not the ones from these lessons — new ones, on live chains, weekly.
Screen structures you have no intention of trading. Reject most of them. The skill in this
method is the rejecting, and it stays sharp only by use.

**Write things down.** What you screened, what you rejected, why. If you eventually deploy, the
log matters more than any single deployment does — that's what Lesson 9 was about, and the
habit is easier to build before there's money involved.

**Decide honestly whether you want the next rung.** Not right now. After a few weeks of
screening, when you know whether this way of looking at the market fits how you actually think.
Some people read this course and find it clarifies something they'd half-seen for years. Others
find it isn't how they want to trade. Both are legitimate outcomes, and the second isn't a
failure of the course or of you.

### The point of all of it

You came in with an instrument that behaved unpredictably. You leave able to look at that same
instrument and see a set of components — from which you build a shape that matches a view you
formed yourself, with a floor you drew, at a price you refused to exceed.

Nobody handed you a signal. Nobody told you what to trade. The whole method fits on one page and
every judgment in it is yours.

That was the point. Not to make you dependent on a better source of answers, but to make the
answers yours to work out.

**Design your thesis. Design your risk. Design your payoff.**

More control. Not more risk.

## Notes — not part of the lesson

**No enrolment ask.** The next rungs are named as capability boundaries, not sold. The line
about deciding "not right now, after a few weeks of screening" is deliberate — a member who
enrols on momentum at the end of a course is the dependency pattern the doctrine rejects, and
delaying the decision costs nothing real.

**"Both are legitimate outcomes"** — explicitly permitting a member to conclude this isn't for
them. This is the capacity-over-dependency principle at its sharpest and Tango should confirm it
survives review. Removing it would make the close ordinary.

**The five "cannot yet" items are specific, not vague.** Each names a concrete missing
capability rather than gesturing at more to learn. That specificity is what makes the list
credible rather than promotional.

**Course complete: eleven pages.** Orientation, L1–L10. All contain inline image markers. The
open items across the corpus — the "Beginner Level" badge on the course page, the campaign
terminology collision, the course name question, and the compliance read on allocation
proportions — remain unresolved and are listed in their respective files.
