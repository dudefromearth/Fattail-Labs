# Video Scripts — Orientation and Module 1

*Pure Options · lesson intro videos*

**Format:** each script has a direction block, a beat sheet, and a clean spoken block. The
spoken block is what goes to HeyGen — plain text, no formatting characters, no symbols that a
TTS engine would read aloud, and numbers written as they should be spoken.

**Consistent across all lessons:** same avatar, same voice settings, same framing. These play
back to back, so any drift in tone or setup is visible as a seam. Use identical Voice Director
settings on every render.

**Runtime target:** forty-five to seventy-five seconds. The videos introduce and frame. The page
teaches.

## Orientation — Pure Options

**Runtime:** ~55 seconds · **Words:** 118
**Tone:** Direct, welcoming, unhurried. This is the first time a member hears the voice.
**Pacing:** Slow on the triad. It is the course's spine and it should land as three separate
statements, not one line.

### Beats

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | The triad | Three strokes resolving into a payoff shape |
| 0:12 | What options are for | Stock's straight line, then built shapes |
| 0:28 | What is assumed | Nothing — hold on speaker |
| 0:38 | What is set down | Four terms, struck through |
| 0:48 | Close | Course title card |

### Spoken text

Design your thesis. Design your risk. Design your payoff.

That is this course. Three decisions, all of them yours, all of them made before you commit a
dollar.

Here is why that is possible with options and with almost nothing else. Buy a stock and you
accept the payoff the stock offers. A straight line, up or down. With options, the shape itself
is something you build.

This course assumes you already know what a call is. It is not an introduction. And four things
are set down here and not picked up again. Share ownership. Assignment. Covered call income.
The wheel.

Not because they are wrong. Because they are built for a longer clock than the one you trade.

Read the page, then open Module One.

## Lesson 1 — What an option is actually for

**Runtime:** ~60 seconds · **Words:** 131
**Tone:** Even and generous. This lesson makes a critical argument about other people's
teaching, and the delivery has to stay respectful or it curdles.
**Pacing:** Slow down on "a long horizon toolkit and a short horizon clock." That is the line
the lesson turns on.
**Critical:** no smirk, no knowing look. If the delivery reads as superior, the lesson fails
regardless of the words.

### Beats

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | The pattern | Four strategies converging on shares |
| 0:15 | Why education goes there | Hold on speaker |
| 0:30 | The clock mismatch | Two horizons on one timeline, to scale |
| 0:45 | What the instrument is for | Built payoff shapes |

### Spoken text

Covered calls. Cash secured puts. The wheel. Assignment.

Look at what those have in common. Every one of them ends with you owning shares. The option is
never the point. It is the toll on the way to holding stock.

There is a reason education goes there, and it is not a bad one. Ownership is easy to
understand, it feels safe, and it can be taught in a single sitting. People run that approach
seriously.

But count the time in it. Covered calls need weeks. The wheel needs months. Every one of those
strategies assumes you have time to be wrong in.

You work in days. Sometimes hours.

You were handed a long horizon toolkit and a short horizon clock. That is the misfit. Not bad
teaching. A mismatched clock.

## Lesson 2 — The option is three dimensional

**Runtime:** ~65 seconds · **Words:** 140
**Tone:** Curious rather than instructive. The member already knows these words, so the delivery
should suggest a second look rather than a first lesson.
**Pacing:** Pause after "they are not definitions." Let the reframe land before continuing.

### Beats

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | You already know this | Hold on speaker |
| 0:12 | The reframe | Price bar splitting into two parts |
| 0:25 | Intrinsic, then extrinsic | Each segment highlighted in turn |
| 0:42 | The room | Three axes building |
| 0:55 | The decay | Decay curve, cliff emphasised |

### Spoken text

You already know an option's price has two parts. Intrinsic and extrinsic. You were probably
handed those words in the first hour of whatever course you took.

They are not definitions. They are the architecture.

Intrinsic value is the real part. What the option would be worth if it expired right now. For
most out of the money options, that is zero, and it stays zero unless price makes the whole trip
to the strike.

Extrinsic value is everything else. It is usually called time value, but the honest name is the
price of possibility. You are paying for what the option might still become.

And that is why an option does not live on a line. It lives in a room, with three walls. Price,
time, and volatility.

Let me show you the room.

## Lesson 3 — The risk graph is a temporal object

**Runtime:** ~60 seconds · **Words:** 128
**Tone:** Quietly revelatory. This lesson gives a member something they have been looking at for
years without seeing.
**Pacing:** Land hard on "there are two lines." Short pause after.

### Beats

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | What you were shown | A single-line payoff diagram |
| 0:10 | There are two lines | Second line appearing above the first |
| 0:25 | The gap is the extrinsic value | Gap shaded and labelled |
| 0:40 | The gap closes | Smooth line falling toward the hard line |
| 0:52 | Why it matters | Hold on speaker |

### Spoken text

You have seen a risk graph. Everyone has. The picture with the bent line, showing what a
position is worth at different prices.

You were probably shown it as a still image. That is about half of what is on the screen.

Because there are two lines. One is hard edged with sharp corners. That is the position at
expiration. The other is smooth and sits above it. That is the position right now, today, which
is the one you actually own.

Now look at the distance between them. That gap is the extrinsic value from the last lesson. Not
a picture of it. It is it.

And here is the part that changes everything. Move the date forward, and the gap closes.

The risk graph is not a picture. It is a film.

## Lesson 4 — The term: all three close in at once

**Runtime:** ~70 seconds · **Words:** 148
**Tone:** Precise. This is the accuracy-critical lesson and the delivery should feel careful.
**Pacing:** Slow and deliberate through the vega section. Do not rush the correction.
**Critical:** the sentence about volatility must be delivered exactly as written. It is the one
thing in the course most likely to be absorbed backwards.

### Beats

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | The term | Two terms compared |
| 0:15 | Time accelerates | Theta rising |
| 0:28 | Price sharpens | Gamma rising |
| 0:40 | Volatility, the correction | Vega falling, distinct colour |
| 0:58 | The summary line | All three plotted together |

### Spoken text

A position with sixty days on it and the same position with two days on it are not the same
trade at different speeds. They behave differently in kind.

That difference has a name. The term. The fixed duration of a trade, from now until it expires.

A long term forgives. A short term does not. And here is why.

Time accelerates. Each remaining day is a bigger fraction of what is left, so decay speeds up as
you approach the end.

Price sharpens. The same ten point move that barely mattered with sixty days left can multiply
or erase a position with two days left.

And volatility does something people get backwards. Its exposure actually shrinks. But there is
no time left to recover from a volatility event.

So. Time accelerates. Price sharpens. Volatility's exposure shrinks while its consequence grows.

## Production notes

**Read the vega beat in Lesson 4 exactly as written.** Two of the three sensitivities rise into
expiry and one falls. Any paraphrase that flattens the three into one pattern teaches something
a member will have to unlearn.

**Lesson 1 is the delivery risk.** The words are careful; the tone can undo them. A member who
recently paid for a course that taught the wheel is being told, gently, that it was aimed at a
different clock. If any part of the read sounds like ridicule, that member is lost. Consider
recording it twice and choosing the warmer take.

**Every script ends pointing forward or into the page.** No summaries and no calls to action
beyond opening the material — the videos frame, the pages teach, and a video that summarises the
page gives a member a reason to skip it.

**Numbers are written as spoken.** "Sixty days" not "60 days," "Module One" not "Module 1." Check
these survive whatever preprocessing HeyGen applies.

**Test two words before committing render credits:** "extrinsic" and "vega." Both appear
constantly and both are pronounceable more than one way. Worth a short test render on the Lesson
2 script before running the set.
