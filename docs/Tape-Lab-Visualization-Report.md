# Ways to look at the opening-move days

**What this is.** A ranking of pictures for the SPX sessions Tape Lab already stores, plus the ones that are now drawn at [http://studiotwo.local:7420/visions](http://studiotwo.local:7420/visions). The counts stay on Studies. This page is about how to see the same days.

**Sample in front of you.** About ten kept sessions on disk, from 2026-08-20 through 2026-09-23, unless you have since fetched the rest from StudioOne. Every rating below assumes that small sample. A picture that needs a hundred days is not “more creative.” It is a picture of noise.

## How a picture earns its place

Four questions, in order. A picture is **High** only if the first two are yes.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Would a trader do something different after seeing it? | A chart that restates a sentence you already have is decoration. |
| Does it stay honest with about ten days? | Empty bins, overlapping lines, and missing straddles have to stay visible. |
| Does it use the chain, or only the index print? | Spot is enough for “did it hold.” The straddle is the fact a price chart cannot show. |
| Can you read it in a few seconds? | If the legend is longer than the finding, the finding is the legend. |

Conventional is not a demerit. A slopegraph is old and, here, better than a novel shape. Unconventional is not a merit. A radar of the same minutes is rounder and worse.

This report will talk about trades. It will not invent a probability from ten days, and it will not tell you to buy or sell a side.

## High — use these

| Picture | Type | Where | What you actually see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain reading | Sentence | Ways to look, top | One line per day: the 10:00 state, whether the close kept that sign, and whether price later left the 10:00 straddle. |
| Morning to the close | Slopegraph | Ways to look | Left dot is 10:00 versus 9:31. Right dot is the close. A line that crosses zero changed color. The slope is the size the Studies bars throw away. |
| Every path, same scale | Overlaid lines | Ways to look | All kept days on one percent scale. The vertical line is 10:00. Left of it is what you could have known. Right of it is what followed. |
| Straddle around the path | Band on a small multiple | Ways to look | The ribbon’s thickness is the 0DTE at-the-money straddle at that snapshot. Missing quote, missing band. Not zero. |

| Left the 10:00 straddle | Yes / no under each tunnel | Ways to look | After 10:00, did price travel farther from that print than half the straddle priced then? On the days now on disk the answer is yes every time, so the yes/no does not separate them. The width of the band still does. |

### How to use the high ones

- **Did the morning hold?** Look at the slopegraph, not the bars, when you care about size. The bars answer “red or green.” The slope answers “by how much, and did it cross.”
- **Don’t explain the morning with the afternoon.** On the fan, cover the right side of the 10:00 line with your hand. That is the information you had. Then look right. The video’s point — the first half hour is noisy, and a move that is still large later is a different fact — is this picture.
- **Premium.** If you sell a straddle, an iron condor, or any short-premium structure, the band is the range you sold, redrawn as the day goes. Leaving it is the loss case. That is ordinary. The page says which of these days left the 10:00 band and which stayed inside. It does not say “sell it.”
- **A debit with the move.** If the morning has already used a large piece of the straddle, a vertical that needs another full straddle of travel is asking the day to pay for the same move twice. Compare the width in the structure calculator with the band on that day’s card. The page still does not pick the call or the put.
- **Fading.** Fading means you need a cross of the 9:31 print. The stripe shows whether that cross happened and when. The Studies reversal table counts it. On this disk there has not been a large down open at 10:00, so there is no fade-rate to quote. Do not borrow the video’s 22 percent and treat it as today’s.

## Medium — useful once the question is clear

| Picture | Type | Why it is not high |
|---|---|---|
| Share of the day above and below the open | Stacked bar | Conventional and readable. It forgets *when* the cross happened. Use it after the stripes. |
| Session clocks | The stripes, bent into a circle | Same facts. Noon sits on the right. Worth it if you think in “the morning versus the afternoon.” Not worth it if you wanted the minute of the cross. |
| Histogram of the 10:00 return | Distribution | Quarter-percent bins. Empty bins stay empty. Ten days are not a bell curve, and the chart does not smooth them into one. |
| Studies close bars | Categorical | The right tool for “of the flat mornings, where did they close,” once a cell has five days. Blind to size and to path. |
| Gap versus the first 30 minutes | Table, already on Studies | The overnight gap and the 9:31-to-10:00 move are different. The table keeps them apart. A scatter of the two would be the same facts with less of the date. |

## Low — true, and weaker than something above

| Picture | Type | Why it loses |
|---|---|---|
| Constellation | Dot, sized by the 10:00 straddle | It is the slopegraph with the “from here to there” removed. The radius is the one new fact, and the tunnel already shows that fact over time. |
| Noise radar | Polar, half-hour wedges | A circle invites a shape. With a handful of days the shape is the sample. The time-of-day line on Studies is the adult version of this question. |

They are on the page so the loss is visible, not so they can be traded.

## What is reasonable, including the ordinary

The long study (January 2012–August 2026) said the first 30 minutes is not confirmation, and that a half-percent move still intact after 60 to 90 minutes finished the same way more often, especially on the downside. That is a sensible prior. It is not a measurement of these ten days.

On the days on disk, one morning was a large up move at 10:00 (2026-09-17) and the close stayed up. Three mornings were only in the middle band, between a quarter and a half of a percent, and all three closed down. Six were flat at 10:00. That is a description. It is not a rate you size a trade from.

Ordinary uses that survive that honesty:

- Wait until the move is still there at 10:30 or 11:00 before you treat it as the day’s direction. The fan is that wait, drawn.
- If you fade, require the stripe to show a cross, and accept that you are taking the smaller side of the long study. Our sample does not yet have the large down opens that study was about.
- If you sell premium, the question is whether the day leaves the band. Read the yes/no under each tunnel.
- If you buy a vertical, set its width against the straddle that is left, not against the move that already printed.

Uses that do not survive:

- Adding size because one line is steep.
- Trading the shape of the radar.
- Calling September 17 the base rate for up opens.
